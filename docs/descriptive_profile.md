# EduInsight — Descriptive Profile

## 1. Overview

This document presents the descriptive analysis of the School Level Data on Official Enrollment in All Grade Levels for School Year 2023–2024. It examines the distribution of schools across geographical areas, sectors, and school classifications, as well as the distribution of learner enrollment across sex, education levels, grade levels, and locations. Statistical summaries and potential outliers are also examined to provide an overview of the dataset's characteristics.

## 2. School Profile Distribution

### 2.1 Schools by Region

The regional distribution shows that Region IV-A has the highest number of schools, with 6,007 records (9.98% of the total), followed by Region III with 5,194 schools (8.63%). Meanwhile, PSO accounts for 33 schools, representing approximately 0.05% of the dataset.

### 2.2 Schools by Division

The distribution of schools by division shows that Iloilo has the highest number of schools, with 1,383 (2.30%), followed by Leyte with 1,363 (2.27%). The other divisions in the top five are Cebu with 1,346 (2.24%), Camarines Sur with 1,281 (2.13%), and Bohol with 1,273 (2.12%). Meanwhile, the lowest counts were recorded in four divisions: Kingdom of Bahrain, East Timor, Sultanate of Oman, and Greece, with one school each.

### 2.3 Schools by Province and Municipality

The provincial distribution shows that Cebu has the highest number of schools among the 87 provinces represented in the dataset, with 2,252 schools (3.74%). It is followed by Pangasinan with 1,743 (2.90%), Leyte with 1,674 (2.78%), and Iloilo with 1,615 (2.68%). Meanwhile, Batanes recorded the lowest number of schools, with 28 (0.05%).

The distribution of schools by municipality shows that Davao City has the highest number of schools, with 656 (1.09%). In contrast, Northern Kabuntalan recorded the lowest count, with one (0.0017%) school. Meanwhile, Dumalner, Carasi, Ivana, Mahatao, Maconacon, Binondo, and Port Area each recorded three schools. Their respective shares round to 0.005% of the total school records.

### 2.4 Schools by Sector

The dataset contains four school sectors: Public, Private, SUCsLUCs, and PSO. Among these, the Public sector accounts for the largest share, with 47,818 schools (79.48%), followed by the Private sector with 12,113 schools (20.13%). Meanwhile, SUCsLUCs accounts for 203 schools (0.34%), while PSO has the smallest representation, with 33 schools (0.05%).

### 2.5 Schools by School Type and Classification

The distribution of schools by type shows that School with no Annexes is the most common category, accounting for 57,296 schools (95.23%). This is followed by Mother school with 2,015 schools (3.35%) and Annex or Extension School(s) with 843 schools (1.40%). Meanwhile, Mobile School(s)/Center(s) has the lowest representation, with only 13 schools (0.02%).

The distribution of schools by classification shows that DepEd Managed schools have the highest representation, with 47,802 schools (79.45%). This is followed by Non-Sectarian schools with 8,558 schools (14.22%) and Sectarian schools with 3,549 schools (5.90%).

The remaining classifications account for smaller portions of the dataset. These include SUC Managed with 181 schools (0.30%), School Abroad with 33 schools (0.05%), LUC with 22 schools (0.04%), DOST Managed with 14 schools (0.02%), Local International School with 6 schools (0.01%), and Other GA Managed with 2 schools (0.0033%).

The middle 50% of schools have enrollment counts ranging from 106 to 452 learners. The minimum enrollment is zero, while the maximum recorded enrollment is 17,167 learners.

### 4.2 Enrollment Distribution

The histogram and boxplot were used to examine the distribution of total enrollment across schools.

The histogram shows a concentration of schools with relatively low enrollment counts, followed by a long right tail representing schools with higher enrollment. This pattern supports the observation that the distribution is positively skewed.

The boxplot also shows numerous observations beyond the upper whisker. These represent schools with enrollment values substantially higher than the central portion of the distribution.

Overall, the visualizations indicate considerable variation in enrollment size across schools. The distribution is not symmetrical, as a relatively small group of schools has much higher enrollment than the majority.

### 4.3 Potential Outliers

The Interquartile Range (IQR) method was used to identify potential outliers in total school enrollment.

The first quartile (Q1) is 106 learners, while the third quartile (Q3) is 452 learners, resulting in an IQR of 346. Based on the IQR method, the upper boundary is 971 learners, while the lower boundary is -413 learners.

Since enrollment counts cannot be negative, the lower boundary does not identify meaningful low-end outliers. Schools with enrollment greater than 971 learners were therefore classified as potential high outliers.

|---|---:|
| Q1 | 106 |
| Q3 | 452 |

A total of 6,367 schools, representing approximately 10.58% of the dataset, exceeded the upper IQR boundary.

Among the schools with the highest enrollment, Batasan Hills National High School recorded the maximum value of 17,167 learners. It was followed by Parañaque National High School Main with 16,847 learners and Davao City National High School with 14,790 learners.

These observations identify unusually high enrollment values relative to the overall distribution. However, they should not automatically be considered erroneous because large enrollment counts may reflect the actual size of the schools.

## 5. Key Findings

The descriptive profiling of the School Level Data on Official Enrollment in All Grade Levels identified several characteristics of the dataset for School Year 2023–2024.

1. **School distribution:** The dataset contains 60,167 school records, with Region IV-A having the highest number of schools. Public schools account for the largest share at 79.48%, while School with no Annexes is the most common school type at 95.23%.

2. **Geographic distribution:** Cebu has the highest number of schools among the provinces represented in the dataset, while Iloilo has the highest school count among divisions. Davao City has the highest number of schools among the municipality categories examined.

3. **Overall enrollment:** The dataset contains 27,081,292 recorded learners. Male enrollment accounts for 51.16%, while female enrollment represents 48.84%.

4. **Education-level distribution:** Elementary has the largest enrollment among the four education levels, accounting for approximately 48.58% of the total. Kindergarten has the smallest share at approximately 7.56%.

5. **Grade-level and strand distribution:** Grade 5 has the highest enrollment among the 29 grade-level and program categories, with 2,309,827 learners. In Senior High School, TVL has the highest enrollment among the reported strands and tracks for both Grade 11 and Grade 12.

6. **Sector distribution of enrollment:** Public schools account for approximately 85.77% of total enrollment, followed by Private schools with 13.78%.

7. **Enrollment variation:** The mean school enrollment is 450.10, while the median is 211. This difference, together with the histogram and boxplot, indicates a positively skewed distribution.

8. **Potential outliers:** The IQR method identified 6,367 schools (10.58%) with enrollment greater than 971 learners. Public schools have the highest number of potential outliers, while PSO has the highest proportion relative to its sector size.

9. **Data grouping validation:** All 58 enrollment columns were successfully assigned to 29 grade-level and program categories exactly once. The grouped enrollment total matches the overall enrollment of 27,081,292.

## 6. Analytical Considerations

The descriptive analysis provides an overview of school distribution and learner enrollment across the dataset. However, several considerations should be taken into account when interpreting the results and using them for further analysis.

1. **Single-year coverage:** The dataset covers School Year 2023–2024 only. Therefore, the analysis cannot establish enrollment growth, decline, or long-term trends across school years.

2. **Aggregated data:** Enrollment values are reported at the school level and are not individual student records. The analysis is limited to aggregated counts and cannot examine individual learner characteristics or transitions.

3. **Geographic representation:** The dataset includes domestic geographic categories as well as PSO records. PSO should be considered separately when interpreting regional and geographic distributions.

4. **Enrollment distribution:** Differences in school enrollment counts may reflect variations in school size. A high enrollment count does not necessarily indicate an error or a higher enrollment rate.

5. **Potential outliers:** The IQR method identifies statistical extremes based on the overall distribution. Potential outliers require further contextual validation before being treated as incorrect records or excluded from analysis.

6. **Zero enrollment:** Some school records have zero total enrollment. These records should be retained during profiling and examined further to understand their context and relationship with the separate Zero Enrollment worksheet.

7. **Geographic and address completeness:** Missing values and placeholder-like entries were identified in the Street Address field, while some Barangay entries were also missing. These limitations should be considered when conducting more detailed location-based analysis.

8. **Zero-enrollment reconciliation:** The DB worksheet's calculated zero-enrollment records do not fully match the regional and sector totals reported in the separate Zero Enrollment worksheet. The difference remains unresolved and requires clarification of the worksheet's coverage or counting criteria before the two sources can be treated as equivalent.

9. **Interpretation of percentages:** Percentages represent shares of the records or enrollment totals within the dataset, depending on the analysis. They should not be interpreted as population-based enrollment rates because the dataset does not provide the corresponding school-age population figures.

Overall, the descriptive profile establishes a baseline understanding of the dataset's structure, school characteristics, enrollment distribution, and statistical properties. These findings can guide subsequent data preparation, analytical exploration, and the development of the EduInsight dashboard.
